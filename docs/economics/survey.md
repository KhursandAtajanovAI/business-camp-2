# What we know about the parents we build for

We did not run our own survey or interviews during the hackathon. This page is honest
secondary data: published surveys with links. No numbers here are ours.

## Published findings

| Finding | Source |
|---|---|
| Health costs tied to pregnancy, birth and care after it average $20,416, of which $2,743 is paid out of pocket (employer plans). | Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker, 2025: https://www.kff.org/health-costs/issue-brief/health-costs-associated-with-pregnancy-childbirth-and-postpartum-care/ |
| 45% of insured working-age adults got a medical bill or copay in the past year for a service they thought insurance should have paid. | Commonwealth Fund, Unforeseen Health Care Bills and Coverage Denials by Health Insurers in the U.S., 2024: https://www.commonwealthfund.org/sites/default/files/2024-07/Gupta_unforeseen_bills_coverage_denials_PR_2024-08-01.pdf |
| Fewer than half of those who saw a billing error challenged it, mostly because they did not know they could. | same Commonwealth Fund report |
| 38% of those who challenged a bill had it reduced or removed. | same Commonwealth Fund report |
| 3,628,934 babies were born in the US in 2024. | CDC NCHS Data Brief 535: https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db535.htm |

## What this tells us

- The pain is real and measured: thousands of dollars out of pocket around one birth.
- Most people do not challenge a bill, and not knowing they can is the main reason. A tool that
  shows what to ask and writes the letter removes exactly that barrier.
- Challenging works often enough (38%) to be worth a $29 try, and we never promise more than that.

## Questions for five short interviews (next step)

1. How many separate papers did you get after the birth, and from whom?
2. Did you check any of them? What made it hard?
3. Did you ever call a billing office about a charge? What happened?
4. What would make you trust a website with a photo of your bill?
5. Would you rather pay once or monthly for help with these papers, and why?

When real answers arrive, they go here with the date, the number of people and their words, without names.
